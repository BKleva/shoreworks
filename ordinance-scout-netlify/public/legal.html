Automated research, not legal advice

Ordinance Scout collects documents from public sources such as municipal websites, ordinance codes and master plans, and from files that users upload. Software then tags passages to townships and summarizes them with labels such as “welcoming,” “discouraging,” “permitted,” “regulated” or “prohibited.”

Those results are a starting point for research and nothing more. They may be incomplete, out of date, attached to the wrong township, or simply wrong. Ordinances are amended often, and the tool may not have the current version. A township showing “No data yet” has not been reviewed. It does not mean the township has no restrictions.

Nothing in the tool is legal advice, a zoning opinion, or a determination that any use is allowed or prohibited at any location. Before making a decision, signing an agreement, applying for a permit or spending money based on anything shown here, confirm it against the current adopted ordinance, with the municipal clerk or zoning officer, and with a licensed New Jersey attorney.

Source documents

Ordinances, plans and other source documents belong to the municipalities, counties and publishers that issued them. They are stored here for internal reference only. The official version published by the municipality always controls.
